Chapter II · Unit 151

The endgame: the weighted prime sum and its remainder, the one-sided criterion (Landau), the choice of the second scale, and the statements and assembly of Theorems A, B and C

note and structured proofs · 29 declarations · 18 structured proofs · 1 note

PrimeError defines the weight λ_{d,M}(ν) = Λ(ν)(1 − (ν/d)) on ν ≡ 1 mod M and the remainder R_{d,M}(x) of its weighted sum, proves the finite-sum identity used by the data, and shows that v ↦ R_{d,M}(e^v) is polar with residues given by orders of zeros of L-functions. OneSidedCriterion turns a one-sided bound R_{d,M}(x) ≤ x^(β−1), for all primes d of a class, into a zero-free half-plane Re s > β (Landau's lemma, applied with a prime d of the class whose quadratic twists of all characters modulo M have no zero in a box to the right of β; the artifact proves that such a d exists from its own zero-density estimate for quadratic twists), and finds in every window [t, t²] a second scale u where R − X·R′ ≤ e^(−t). ShortAndEnough defines the main amount, the remainder rem(D) and the index, bounds the height per row by the choice of the level prime, writes rem(D) through R_{d,N₀} and R_{d,1}, and proves Theorem A (short forms) and Theorem B (enough forms) from the seesaw identity, the size and divisibility of the chains of lifts, and the filling theorem. LongForms proves Theorem C (integral forms are long) by heights and the product formula, and combines A and B into one family.
