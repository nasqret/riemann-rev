Unit 148Translates of Poincaré series fill the rows: a Petersson-type second moment along a Bessel subgroup

note and structured proofs · 496 declarations · 179 structured proofs

Unit 148 (the folder New/Filling) proves the filling theorem used for Theorem B (enough forms): for admissible data D, every nonzero constant c and every subspace F of the rows, some element s of the finite level group \mathsf{G} = \prod \mathrm{GL} over the residue fields at the chosen places above g = q_0 q_1 makes the preimage of F under the composite (|\mathsf{G}| e_0 s) \circ \mathcal{P} \circ (c \cdot E) have dimension at most \bigl((1 + \dim F/\dim \text{rows})/2 + b^{-2}\bigr) \cdot \dim \text{Col}, where \mathcal{P} is the Poincaré series built from cut Bessel functions along the Bessel subgroup S = \mathsf{U}\mathsf{H} of the unitary group of V = H^\varrho \oplus X_0 \oplus Ez, e_0 the projection to the rows and E the projection onto the ramified columns (Data.ramifiedColumns_fill_rows). The proof is a second-moment (Petersson-type) argument: the mean over s of the squared Hilbert–Schmidt norm of \mathcal{P}^* s \mathcal{P} is unfolded into sums of amplitudes over rational elements \gamma grouped by double cosets (classes c, c') and by their reductions s modulo g; amplitude sums vanish unless the unipotent characters agree, degenerate terms and incoherent pairs are negligible by discriminant gains, lattice counts and a weighted Schur test, the main term is bounded nearly sharply by positive kernels of local Bessel integrals at finitely many places and a sieve (one-sided Poisson summation), and a variance inequality turns the moment bound into the spreading statement. Chunk 148-a holds the set-up and the first layer: the record Filling of the data and its standing hypotheses IsAdmissible; abstract sieve systems and Bessel systems; the groups \mathsf{U}, \mathsf{U}^0, \mathsf{H}, \mathsf{S}, the classes, terms, amplitudes and constants; dictionaries between rational, finite adelic and real coordinates; links of frames (Lagrangian, invariant under \mathsf{U} on both sides); Siegel's lemma for hermitian lattices and a convergent height zeta function of subspaces; strong approximation for \mathsf{U}; normal forms and levels of terms; the bound of the index of a determinant of pairings by agreeing characters; box partitions (cutoffs) for the arithmetic unipotent groups \mathsf{U}_c(g) and the frame-counting bound; the exact covolume of \mathsf{U}_c(g); and convergence, change of weight, moves, vanishing for disagreeing characters and the identity term of the amplitude sums.

Unit 132The analysis at the real places: tower measures, gauges, open-cell charts and Plancherel bounds

note and structured proofs · 238 declarations · 108 structured proofs

Unit 132 (New/RealPlace) is the analysis at the real places that Theorem B (enough forms) needs. Its first chunk (132-a) sets up the tower method: sizes and gauges (filtrations B_0 \subset B_1 \subset \cdots of the ring that bound the entries of group elements near a frame), a level (measures \theta_l on the floors of the unitary group of a split hermitian space, whose convolution is a Haar measure), pairing kernels averaged floor by floor, and the weight, fibre and Plancherel bounds on each floor; at the complex place it builds the level and the gauge explicitly, proves that the tower measure is a constant multiple of Haar measure with the constant controlled through an open-cell double coset chart (Jacobian and injectivity), and bounds integrals of Bessel functions over that chart. The second chunk (132-b) proves from these tools the local theorem (for a general place, then summed over shells at \mathbb{C}), the band limits, and the open-cell, cut-off and volume-kernel bounds.

Unit 133Discrete series coefficients near 1, Laplace's method, and explicitly large Bessel functions

note and structured proofs · 37 declarations · 17 structured proofs

Unit 133 (New/DiscreteSeries) supplies the archimedean lower bounds that Theorem B (enough forms) and the restriction periods of the exchange step need. Near the identity a discrete series coefficient \psi_l(g) of U(p, q) (Flensted-Jensen's function) is an explicit phase \exp\operatorname{tr}(\operatorname{diag}(\Lambda(l))\operatorname{lg}(g - 1)) times a real factor \kappa \in [0, 1], with a uniform error (dsCoeff_gaussian_shape, from a second-order expansion of principal minors, MinorExpansion). For a 'two-factor' integrand \theta(s) = \psi_l(j_1(s)) \cdot \operatorname{conj} \psi_k(j_2(s)) \cdot e^{-\varphi(s)} on a group S (IsTwoFactor: steep weights, a purely imaginary additive phase, U(X) embedded diagonally) Laplace's method gives the explicit lower bound \nu\{\lVert j_1(s) - 1\rVert \le 1\} \le \beta^{20(|n|+|m|)^2} \cdot \left|\int \theta \, d\nu\right| (IsTwoFactor.laplace_method). Two instances are built: the restriction r \mapsto (r, \omega^{-1}(r \oplus 1)\omega) with \omega from Lagrange weights (IsSteep.exists_isTwoFactor_restriction), and the Bessel graph S = H\mathsf{U} at a point x of controlled size (IsSteep.exists_isTwoFactor_besselPairHom); from the latter, Bessel functions with a peak at x are constructed (exists_isBesselFunction, HasPeakAt). The unit also proves the unfolding of the period of a deep-level Poincaré series against an automorphic form (norm_poincare_period_sub_le).
