lemma Filling.hasSum_amplitudeSum_change_of_weight

What it saysChange of weight. Under the standing hypotheses, for filling data S over a stably unramified CM number field E with its CM type (\mathfrak{s} implicit), the side \sigma, a stable set T (hT; implicit T), classes c,c',s\in\mathsf{G} and a weight \varphi(\gamma,y)\in[0,\infty] with \sum_{(u_1,u_2)\in\mathsf{U}_c(g)\times\mathsf{U}_{c'}(g)}\varphi(u_1\gamma u_2^{-1},\mathtt{ratToInf}(u_1)\cdot y)=1 for every \gamma\in X=T\cap termsOver(c,s,c') and every y (h), each \varphi(\gamma,\cdot) measurable (hm, by default by measurability): the integrals \int\varphi(\gamma,y)\cdotbesselKernel(\gamma,y)dy,\gamma\in X, have sum A_T(c,s,c')= amplitudeSum(S,T,c,s,c'), each integrand is integrable, and \sum_{\gamma\in X}\int^{-}\varphi(\gamma,y)\cdot\lVert\operatorname{kernel}_{\sigma}-\mathtt{kernel\_cut}\rVert\le\nu\cdot b^{-k^{3}f}/2.
The argument in brief\Delta=\mathsf{U}_c(g)\times\mathsf{U}_{c'}(g) acts on X\times\prod G_1 preserving counting \times Haar measure and the kernel; for any cutoff \beta of \Delta the \beta-weighted integrals are those of the orbit measure, independent of \beta; compare \varphi with the cutoff \beta_c(y)\beta_{c'}(\gamma^{-1}y) that defines the amplitudes.
