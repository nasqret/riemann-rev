Unit 155The five final theorems: the Riemann hypothesis and the generalised Riemann hypothesis for Dirichlet L-functions

Unit 153The statements: the Riemann hypothesis and the generalised Riemann hypothesis for Dirichlet L-functions, in three forms

note and structured proofs · 4 declarations

Four propositions, stated with Mathlib's ζ and Dirichlet L-functions only (the module imports nothing but Mathlib): zeros of ζ off the trivial zeros and off s = 1 lie on Re s = 1/2; no Dirichlet L-function vanishes on Re s > 1/2 (the point s = 1 of a principal character excepted); zeros of Dirichlet L-functions in the open strip 0 < Re s < 1 lie on Re s = 1/2; the same for primitive characters. They are the types of the five theorems of Main (unit 155).

Unit 154The inequality (⋆), the one-sided bound for the remainder of the weighted prime sum, the generalised Riemann hypothesis, and the passage to the critical line

note and structured proofs · 5 declarations · 5 structured proofs

The top of the argument. (⋆): for admissible data D, m·rem ≤ (log b)²·𝓑, from Theorems A–B (a long independent family of rows of small index with short lifts) against Theorem C (the covolume is at least the main amount). The one-sided bound: R_{d,N₀}(x) ≤ x^(8ε − 1/2) for large x, by choosing the second scale x₁ in a window where R_{N₀} − (towerDeg/#thick)·R₁ is tiny and writing R_{N₀}(x) through rem. GRH: a zero right of 1/2 is excluded by the one-sided criterion (Landau) applied to the class N₀/2 − 1 modulo N₀ = 18480q. Then the functional equation gives the zeros on the critical line, for Dirichlet L-functions and for ζ.
