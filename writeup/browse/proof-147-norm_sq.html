lemma Data.TameChar.IsIntegralMeasure.norm_sq

What it saysFor P, D and a tame character \vartheta: if F is an integral measure for \vartheta (Data.TameChar.IsIntegralMeasure), then so is \psi \mapsto |F(\psi)|^{2}.
The argument in brief|F|^{2} = F\cdot \overline{F} is the integral measure on the pairs (\alpha, \beta) with points u_{\alpha}\cdot u_{\beta}^{-1} and coefficients c_{\alpha}\cdot \overline{c}_{\beta}.

    Given F(\vartheta ') = \sum c_{\alpha} \vartheta '(u_{\alpha}), take the points u_{\alpha}\cdot u_{\beta}^{-1} and the coefficients c_{\alpha}\cdot \operatorname{conj}(c_{\beta}) over pairs (\alpha, \beta): they are integral away from Q (products of such numbers, conjugation preserving integrality at l), and |F(\vartheta ')|^{2} = F(\vartheta ')\cdot \operatorname{conj} F(\vartheta ') = \sum_{\alpha,\beta} c_{\alpha} \operatorname{conj}(c_{\beta}) \vartheta '(u_{\alpha} u_{\beta}^{-1}), because \vartheta ' takes values on the unit circle (\operatorname{conj} \vartheta '(u) = \vartheta '(u)^{-1}).
