lemma IsSplitLevel.LiftLaw.eq_zero_of_smul_invariant

What it saysLet L be a lift law for \pi, \pi' with constant \gamma (H; P a split level for the prime N, instance \Lambda), \varphi \in \operatorname{ramified}(\pi) (h), w \in P, and f a function on residue matrices such that f(c\cdot T) = f(T) for every T and every c \in (\mathbb{Z}/N)^{P} with the property that c\cdot t = y \iff t = y for all t, y \in (\mathbb{Z}/N)^{P} with y_w = 0 (that is, c_v = 1 at the places v \ne w and c_w \ne 0) (a). Then L[liftFn f]\varphi = 0.
The argument in brief\varphi = \psi - \pi(x)\psi for a scalar x \equiv 1 off w; L[f] does not see \pi(x) because f is invariant under the scaling by \overline{x}.

    Since \varphi is ramified, for the place w there are an integral unit x with \operatorname{reduction}(x)_{v} = 1 for v \ne w (e) and a vector \psi with \varphi = (1 - \pi(x\cdot1))\psi (b).

  2. 2deductionlines 201–202

    By the equivariance of the law (integral_equivariant with a = x\cdot1, b = 1), L[liftFn f](\pi(x)\psi) = L[liftFn(T \mapsto f(\overline{x}\cdot T))]\psi = L[liftFn f]\psi, since c = \overline{x} satisfies the hypothesis of a: for t, y with y_w = 0, c\cdot t = y \iff t = y (c_v = 1 for v \ne w, and c_w is a unit; checked place by place). Hence L[liftFn f]\varphi = L[liftFn f]\psi - L[liftFn f]\psi = 0.
