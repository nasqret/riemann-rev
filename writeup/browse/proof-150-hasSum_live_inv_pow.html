lemma hasSum_live_inv_pow

What it saysLet d and p be primes (instances Fact d.Prime, Fact p.Prime) with \chi_K(p) = (p/d) \ne 1, and let c \in \mathbb{R}. Then the series \sum_{e \in \mathbb{N}} [e \ge 1 and (e odd or p = d)]\cdot c/p^e converges to (2/(1 - \chi_K(p)))\cdot c\cdot(1/(p - 1) - \rho_p), where \rho_p = nonsplitWeight d p (1/(p^2 - 1) \text{ if } (p/d) = -1, 1/(2(p - 1)) \text{ if } p = d).
The argument in briefWith \chi = \chi_K(p), write the e-th term as (c/(1 - \chi))\cdot(p^{-e} - (\chi /p)^e) (the multiplier 2c/(1 - \chi) times half the difference of the two geometric terms); it vanishes at e = 0. Sum the two geometric series in the two cases \chi = -1 (multiplier c/2: the odd terms, sum cp/(p^2 - 1)) and \chi = 0, p = d (multiplier c: all positive terms, sum c/(p - 1)).

    Both geometric series converge (|1/p| < 1, |\chi /p| < 1, \chi = \chi_K(p)); the convert matches the given sum with 2c/(1 - \chi) times half their difference, so the e-th term to identify is (c/(1 - \chi))\cdot(p^{-e} - (\chi /p)^e), which is 0 at e = 0. Since \chi \ne 1, \chi = -1 or p = d with \chi = 0: in the first case the term is (c/2)(1 - (-1)^e)p^{-e}, i.e. c/p^e for odd e and 0 for even e, and the odd terms sum to cp/(p^2 - 1) = c\cdot(1/(p-1) - 1/(p^2-1)) = (2/2)\cdot c\cdot(1/(p-1) - \rho_p); in the second the term is c/p^e for every e \ge 1 (the multiplier is c, not c/2) and the sum is c/(p - 1) = 2c\cdot(1/(p-1) - 1/(2(p-1))). The case split, simp_all and grind check these identities term by term and for the sums.
