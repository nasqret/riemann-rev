lemma one_square

What it saysOne square: lift then descent is proportional to descent then lift. Let E be a field which is a CM number field with a CM type and carries Weil data; n, n', n_{1}, n_{2} finite index types with signs s, s', s_{1}, s_{2}; A' : Space(E, n, s), A : Space(E, n', s'), U_{1} : Space(E, n_{1}, s_{1}), U_{2} : Space(E, n_{2}, s_{2}). Let I be an index set with families of characters a(i, \cdot), b(i, \cdot), \nu(i), moduli \mathfrak{m}(i) and local characters \chi(i), and suppose four edge families (IsEdgeFamily): h_{2} for b from k_{2} to k in direction d with datum \Phi_{2}, h_{1} for a from k_{1} to k in direction \neg d with \Phi_{1}, h_{3} for a from k' to k_{2} in direction \neg d with \Phi_{2}', h_{4} for b from k' to k_{1} in direction d with \Phi_{1}'. Let \sigma be an identification of index sets, and assume negCount(s_{1} \oplus s_{2}) = negCount(s') + negCount(s) (hs), |n'| = |n| + 2e (he), 2\cdotnegCount(s_{1} \oplus s_{2}) + 2 \le |n_{1} \sqcup n_{2}| (hn), weights \mu with |n_{1} \sqcup n_{2}| \le 2\mu(v) + 2\cdotnegCount(s_{1} \oplus s_{2}) at every infinite place (h\mu), each \nu(i) tame for \mathfrak{m}(i) with weights \mu and character \chi(i) (ht), and a(i, p)\cdot b(i, q) = rankTwist(\nu(i)^{-1}, n_{1} \sqcup n_{2}) whenever |p| + |q| = |n_{1}| + |n_{2}| (hE). For a matrix x write u(i, x) = modDescent(\cdot, A, b(i), \Phi_{2}, \delta_{x_{2}}) \circ modLift(U_{1}, A, a(i), \Phi_{1}, \delta_{x_{1}}) (lift from U_{1} to A, then descent to U_{2}, with point data at the column blocks x_{1}, x_{2} of x), and for y write v(i, y) = modLift(A', U_{2}, a(i), \Phi_{2}', \delta_{y_{2}}) \circ modDescent(\cdot, \cdot, b(i), \Phi_{1}', \delta_{y_{1}}) (descent from U_{1} to A', then lift to U_{2}, with point data at the row blocks of y). There are C and \kappa such that every i has c \ne 0 with |c|^{2} = C\cdotlValueProd(places(\mathfrak{m}(i)), \nu(i), e) and, for every form f on U_{1} and every linear functional l, SquareLaw(\chi(i), \sigma, \kappa /c, x \mapsto l(u(i, x)(f)), y \mapsto l(v(i, y)(f))). (\kappa is not shown to be non-zero.)
The argument in briefThe exchange theorem for (U_{1} \oplus U_{2}, A, A') with polynomial real data; the constant \kappa is obtained by approximating its real datum by polynomial vectors (the integrals lie in a fixed line, closed); the equivariances of the two compositions give the first two laws of SquareLaw, and the partner identity is the exchange identity transported through Fubini and the adjointness of lift and descent.
