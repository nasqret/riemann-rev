lemma IsPolar.eventually_moment_le

What it saysFor functions E : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R} and r : \mathbb{C} \to \mathbb{C} with IsPolar(E, r) (h): for every R > 3 close enough to 3 (Lean's \forall^{f} R in \mathcal{N}[>] 3: there is \varepsilon > 0 such that what follows holds for every R \in (3, 3 + \varepsilon)), for every sufficiently large real t (the threshold may depend on R, not on k): |E(v)| \le t\cdot e^{v} for every v \ge 0, and for every natural number k the function F_k(v) = v^{k}\cdot e^{-3v}\cdot E(v) (Lean's local let F v := … of the statement) is integrable on (0, \infty) and |\int_{0}^\infty F_k(v) dv| \le k!\cdot t/R^{k}.
The argument in briefThe moments of e^{-3v}E are derivatives of \Phi at 4 (differentiation under the integral sign); \Phi's Taylor series at 4 converges at 4 + R because (s - 1)\Phi is analytic on a disc of radius > 3 about 4.
