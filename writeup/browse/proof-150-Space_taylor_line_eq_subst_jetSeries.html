lemma Space.taylor_line_eq_subst_jetSeries

What it saysLet E be a field with a CM type and Weil data, n a finite index set, V a hermitian space over E of rank n with all signs +1, f \in \mathcal{W}_f (Space.finitePart), a \in \mathbb{C} and v \colon \Phi \times n \to \mathbb{C} (a vector in Fock coordinates, \Phi the infinite places of E). Then the Taylor series at 0 of the one-variable function u \mapsto \Theta_f(u\cdot a\cdot v) (\Theta_f = Space.entireTheta f) equals the jet series T_a f (Space.jetSeries a f) with every variable z_i replaced by v_i\cdot u (substitution of the linear series (v_i\cdot u)_i).
The argument in briefCompare the coefficients of u^k. \Theta_f(u\cdot a\cdot v) is an integral of f against \exp(u\cdot L(w)) with L the linear form w \mapsto a\sum_p\langle w_p, v_p\rangle; expanding the exponential and exchanging sum and integral (dominated convergence) gives its power series; k! times the k-th coefficient of either side is the jet of T_a f along (\sum v_i z_i)^k: jet((\sum_i v_i z_i)^k, F) = k!\cdot [u^k]F(u\cdot v) (a jet along a monomial z^\alpha carries the factor \alpha !, ZerosCostDegree.lean 162).

  3. 3factlines 27–35

    Two facts. (a) The function t \mapsto \Theta_f(t\cdot a\cdot v) has at 0 the power series with coefficients \int \operatorname{realVac}(w)\cdot f(\operatorname{realVec} w)\cdot L(w)^k/k! d\mu(w): its value at every t is the integral of \operatorname{realVac}(w) f(\operatorname{realVec} w) e^{tL(w)}, and the exponential series can be summed under the integral by dominated convergence (|L(w)| \le \lVert L\rVert \lVert w\rVert, \int e^{c\lVert w\rVert }\cdot \operatorname{realVac} integrable); hence k! times its k-th Taylor coefficient is that integral. (b) k! times the coefficient of u^k of the substituted series T_a f(v\cdot u) equals the jet of T_a f along the polynomial (\sum_i v_i z_i)^k (jet_subst_linearSeries turns the jet of the substituted series along X^k, which is k!\cdot [u^k], into that jet).
