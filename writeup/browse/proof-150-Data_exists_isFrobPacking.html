lemma Data.exists_isFrobPacking

What it saysFor data D, a prime p with \chi_K(p) \ne 1 and n : places \to \mathbb{N} with w(\sigma) \le n_\sigma \le w(\sigma) + x^{42} for every infinite place \sigma (w = Data.nominalWeight), there are i, y : places \to \mathbb{N} with \operatorname{IsFrobPacking}(p, n, i, y) such that, with L = \operatorname{forcedPart}(D, p) + \sum_s v_p(\operatorname{depthLossInt}(D, n_s)), either (\chi_K(p) = -1, i \equiv 1 \text{ and } \sum_s (v_p(n_s!) - y_s) \le L) or \sum_s ((1/(p-1) - \rho_p)\cdot n_s + 4\cdot b_2 - y_s) \le L (sums over all infinite places s, real numbers; \rho_p = \operatorname{nonsplitWeight}(P.d, p), b_2 = D.b₂). The disjunction is the body of the let L := … of the statement, which goes on to line 38 (the census cut it at line 36).
The argument in briefIf k < p (then \chi_K(p) = -1): take i \equiv 1 and y_s = \min(n_{(-p)s}, \lfloor(n_s \mathbin{\dot-} c)/p\rfloor), c = \lceil 60b/k\rceil (\mathbin{\dot-} the truncated subtraction of \mathbb{N}); Legendre's formula and the ascending factorial in depthLossInt give the first alternative. If p \le k: for a place s such that some odd e < s (the exponent of the data) with p \ne d has s and (-p)^e\cdot s thick, that e is unique (the three-term condition of the data); take i_s = e and y_s = \lfloor n_s/p^e\rfloor; for every other place, thick places without such a partner included, y_s = 0. Comparing termwise the series of hasSum_live_inv_pow with the local ask, the chosen term and a geometric series gives the second alternative.
