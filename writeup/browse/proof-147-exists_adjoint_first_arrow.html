lemma IsEdgeFamily.exists_adjoint_first_arrow

What it saysLet E be a field which is a CM number field with a CM type and carries Weil data; N, N' finite index types, signs s, s', S : Space(E, N, s), T : Space(E, N', s'); I an index type and a(i, \cdot) characters. Suppose h: an edge family for a from the kernel pair (l, s) to k' in direction false with real datum \Phi (IsEdgeFamily); let i \in I, \mathfrak{m} a modulus and \Psi a point function. Put b(i, n) = a(i, n)^{-1}\cdot \upsilon^{|n|}. If there is an edge family for b from the kernel pair (l, -s) to k' in direction true with datum \Phi ', then there is \Psi ' such that for all forms y (on S) and x (on T): \langle x, modLift(S, T, a(i), \Phi, \Psi)(y)\rangle = \langlemodDescent(S.neg, T, b(i), \Phi ', \Psi ')(x), formNegEquiv(S, l)(y)\rangle: the lift along the first edge is the adjoint of the descent along the second, through formNegEquiv.
The argument in briefThe kernel of the opposite space is a constant multiple of the conjugate kernel (exists_modKernel_neg_left), and the Petersson product of descents is given by petersson_descent_eq; \Psi ' = c\cdot \operatorname{conj}(\kappa)\cdot(that datum).
