lemma exists_integrable_kernel_bound

What it saysFor E, n', n_{1}, n_{2}, signs and spaces A, U_{1}, U_{2} as in the section, forms y on U_{1} and y' on U_{2} that are automorphic and rapidly decreasing (r, r'), characters b_{1}, b_{2} of U(1)(\mathbb{A}) and a kernel K(v, g, z) (v a Fock vector for the signs s, g a point of U(U_{1} \oplus U_{2}), z a point of U(A)) such that (h) there are B, N, C with |K(v, g, z)| \le C\cdot \text{weightedNorm}_N(v)\cdotheight(g)^{B} whenever \text{weightedNorm}_N(v) < \infty, and (hK) each K(v, \cdot, \cdot) is continuous: there are N and C such that for every v with \text{weightedNorm}_N(v) < \infty the function F(q) = y(q_{11})\cdot y'(q_{12})\cdot(b_{1}(\det q_{11})\cdot b_{2}(\det q_{12}))^{-1}\cdot K(v, sum(q_{1}), q_{2}) is integrable over [U(U_{1})] \times [U(U_{2})] \times [U(A)] and |F(q)| \le C\cdot \text{weightedNorm}_N(v) for all q.
The argument in briefThe rapid decay of y, y' absorbs the polynomial growth of K in the height; F is then bounded and continuous on a finite measure space.
