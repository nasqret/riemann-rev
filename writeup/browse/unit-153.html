Chapter I · Unit 153

The statements: the Riemann hypothesis and the generalised Riemann hypothesis for Dirichlet L-functions, in three forms

note and structured proofs · 4 declarations · 1 note

Four propositions, stated with Mathlib's \zeta and Dirichlet L-functions only (the module imports nothing but Mathlib): zeros of \zeta off the trivial zeros and off s = 1 lie on \operatorname{Re} s = 1/2; no Dirichlet L-function vanishes on \operatorname{Re} s > 1/2 (the point s = 1 of a principal character excepted); zeros of Dirichlet L-functions in the open strip 0 < \operatorname{Re} s < 1 lie on \operatorname{Re} s = 1/2; the same for primitive characters. They are the types of the five theorems of Main (unit 155).

What it holds · 4 declarations

  • defRiemannZetaZerosOnLinelines 3–4argument

    The proposition RiemannZetaZerosOnLine (the Riemann hypothesis): for every complex number s, if \zeta(s) = 0, if s is not of the form -2(n + 1) for any natural number n (that is, s is none of the trivial zeros -2, -4, -6, \ldots), and if s \ne 1, then \operatorname{Re} s = 1/2. Here \zeta is Mathlib's riemannZeta, the meromorphic continuation of \sum_{n\ge 1} n^{-s} to \mathbb{C}; Mathlib defines it at every complex number, also at the pole s = 1, where it takes a conventional finite value.

  • defDirichletLNonvanishingRightOfHalflines 6–8argument

    The proposition DirichletLNonvanishingRightOfHalf (the generalised Riemann hypothesis for Dirichlet L-functions, in half-plane form): for every natural number N with N \ne 0 (Lean's instance argument NeZero N), every Dirichlet character \chi modulo N with complex values, and every complex number s with 1/2 < \operatorname{Re} s, if \chi is not the principal character modulo N or s \ne 1, then L(s, \chi) \ne 0. Here L(s, \chi) is Mathlib's DirichletCharacter.LFunction χ s, the meromorphic continuation of \sum_{n\ge 1} \chi(n) n^{-s} (for the principal character it has a pole at s = 1, where Mathlib assigns a conventional value); a Dirichlet character modulo N is Mathlib's DirichletCharacter ℂ N, a multiplicative map \mathbb{Z}/N \to \mathbb{C} that vanishes off the units, and 1 is the principal character modulo N (1 on the units, 0 elsewhere).

From Lean's own library · 4 facts

  • riemannZeta

    The Riemann zeta function ζ : ℂ → ℂ: for Re s > 1 the sum ∑_{n≥1} n^{−s}, continued meromorphically to ℂ with a simple pole at s = 1 (Mathlib defines it as the even Hurwitz zeta function at 0); as a Lean function it is defined at every point, also at s = 1.

  • DirichletCharacter

    A Dirichlet character modulo N with values in R: a multiplicative character of ℤ/N (a multiplicative map ℤ/N → R sending non-units to 0); 1 is the principal character. It is evaluated at integers and natural numbers through the reduction to ℤ/N.

  • DirichletCharacter.LFunction

    For N ≠ 0 and a Dirichlet character χ modulo N with complex values, s ↦ L(s, χ): for Re s > 1 the series ∑_{n≥1} χ(n)n^{−s}, and its meromorphic continuation elsewhere (entire unless χ is principal; then a simple pole at s = 1, where the Lean function takes a conventional value).

  • DirichletCharacter.IsPrimitive

    χ modulo N is primitive when its conductor (the least modulus through which it factors) equals N.
