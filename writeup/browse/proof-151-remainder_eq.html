lemma Data.remainder_eq

What it saysThe remainder in terms of prime sums. For parameters P (N_{0} = P.N_{0}, d = P.d) and admissible data D for P (Lean's Data P): \operatorname{rem}(D) = a_D\cdot(\#\operatorname{thick}(D)\cdot(R_{d,N_{0}}(x_D) - R_{d,N_{0}}(x_{1,D})) + \#\operatorname{tops}(D)\cdot R_{d,1}(x_{1,D})), where \#\operatorname{thick}(D) and \#\operatorname{tops}(D) count the thick places and the tops; R_{d,M}(x) = primeError d M x = \sum_{1\le\nu\le x} \lambda_{d,M}(\nu)(1/\nu - 1/x) - \log x/\varphi(M) - \operatorname{Re} G_{d,M}(1).
The argument in briefSum the asks over the tops: the thick places are met exactly when \nu \equiv 1 mod N_{0}; evaluate the sums by primeError_eq_sum at x and x_{1}; the main terms are those of \#\mathrm{tops}\cdotAsk_main.

    A: for every natural \nu and real b: \#{\tau \in \operatorname{tops}(D) : (-\nu)\cdot\tau \in \operatorname{thick}(D)}\cdot(\lambda_{d,1}(\nu)\cdot b) = \#\operatorname{thick}(D)\cdot(\lambda_{d,N_{0}}(\nu)\cdot b). Here (-\nu)\cdot\tau is \tau times the place named by -\nu (Data.placeOfInt): the class of -\nu mod N_{1} when \nu is prime to N_{1}, and the identity place when it is not (for instance \nu = 0). Put \sigma = (-\nu)\cdot\tau: the count is the number of thick places \sigma such that \sigma divided by the place named by -\nu is a top (class \equiv 1 mod N_{0}). Thick places have class \equiv -1 mod N_{0} (Data.placeClass_of_thick), so for a thick \sigma this holds iff the place named by -\nu has class \equiv -1 mod N_{0}, which by the exact test Parameters.placeClass_placeOfInt_eq_neg_one (valid for every integer, the identity convention included) holds iff \nu \equiv 1 mod N_{0} — for all thick \sigma or for none. So if \nu \equiv 1 mod N_{0} the count is \#\operatorname{thick}(D) and \lambda_{d,1}(\nu) = \lambda_{d,N_{0}}(\nu); otherwise the count is 0 and \lambda_{d,N_{0}}(\nu) = 0. In particular, for \nu not prime to N_{1} the translation is the identity, it keeps the tops (class 1 mod N_{0}) outside the thick set, and both sides are 0. (These terms vanish also for a reason this step does not use: such a \nu with \Lambda(\nu) \ne 0 is a power of a prime p \mid N_{1}, and \chi_d(p) = 1 for these p, Different.lean 54–58, so \lambda_{d,1}(\nu) = 0.)
