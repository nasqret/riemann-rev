Structured proof · Unit 138

lemma exists_eventually_no_eigenvalue_one

What it saysFor admissible data D, a prime \ell, a continuous T : \operatorname{Gal}(\Omega /E) \to M_n(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell ), elements \delta _i \in \operatorname{Gal}(\Omega /E) (i \in \mathbb{N}), y \in \overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell and a matrix A with T(\delta _i) = y^i\cdot A for every i (h) and \lVert y^j - 1\rVert = 1 for 1 \le j \le 4|n| + 2 (hy): for some i, every \varphi in a neighbourhood of \delta _i has \lVert \operatorname{charpoly}(T(\varphi ))(1)\rVert \ge 1, i.e. \lVert \det (1 - T(\varphi ))\rVert \ge 1 — a lower bound on the norm only (the statement gives no upper bound, so \det (1 - T(\varphi )) need not be a unit).
The argument in briefOtherwise, for each i \le |n|, some eigenvalue \lambda _i of A has \lVert 1 - y^i\lambda _i\rVert < 1: \det (1 - y^{iA}) is the product of the 1 - y^i\lambda over the eigenvalues \lambda of A, and if every factor had norm \ge 1, then \lVert \det (1 - T(\varphi ))\rVert \ge 1 near \delta _i (T continuous, T(\delta _i) = y^{iA}). Then \lVert y^i\lambda _i\rVert = 1, and the \lambda _i are pairwise distinct because \lVert y^{j-i} - 1\rVert = 1 for 1 \le j - i \le |n|: |n| + 1 distinct roots of a characteristic polynomial of degree |n|, a contradiction. No integrality of A or of T is used: the statement gives none.
