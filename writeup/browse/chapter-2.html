From a one-sided bound for primes to a zero-free half-plane

1 unit · 1 note, 36 pages · 18 structured proofs

Unit 151The endgame: the weighted prime sum and its remainder, the one-sided criterion (Landau), the choice of the second scale, and the statements and assembly of Theorems A, B and C

note and structured proofs · 29 declarations · 18 structured proofs

PrimeError defines the weight \lambda_{d,M}(\nu) = \Lambda(\nu)(1 - (\nu/d)) on \nu \equiv 1 \bmod M and the remainder R_{d,M}(x) of its weighted sum, proves the finite-sum identity used by the data, and shows that v \mapsto R_{d,M}(e^v) is polar with residues given by orders of zeros of L-functions. OneSidedCriterion turns a one-sided bound R_{d,M}(x) \le x^{\beta-1}, for all primes d of a class, into a zero-free half-plane \operatorname{Re} s > \beta (Landau's lemma, applied with a prime d of the class whose quadratic twists of all characters modulo M have no zero in a box to the right of \beta; the artifact proves that such a d exists from its own zero-density estimate for quadratic twists), and finds in every window [t, t^2] a second scale u where R - X \cdot R' \le e^{-t}. ShortAndEnough defines the main amount, the remainder \operatorname{rem}(D) and the index, bounds the height per row by the choice of the level prime, writes \operatorname{rem}(D) through R_{d,N_0} and R_{d,1}, and proves Theorem A (short forms) and Theorem B (enough forms) from the seesaw identity, the size and divisibility of the chains of lifts, and the filling theorem. LongForms proves Theorem C (integral forms are long) by heights and the product formula, and combines A and B into one family.
