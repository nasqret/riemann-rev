lemma BaseLevel.weightedSum_ext

What it saysTesting on eigenvectors. Let \ell, \iota_\ell \colon \mathbb{C} \cong \overline{Q}_\ell, the CM field E \subset \Omega, the rank type n, V, \kappa, J, \nu, c, the base datum D and the Taylor–Wiles places Q (with places w_j, j \in J; write w_Q for the family j \mapsto w_j) be as in the setting. Let j \in J, r, s \in \operatorname{heckeAlgebra}(D, w_Q), \Gamma a finite group, \rho \colon \Gamma \to \operatorname{integralEnd}(D, w_Q) a homomorphism into the lattice-preserving operators on \operatorname{formsOfLevel}(D, w_Q) with \rho(g) \in \operatorname{localOps}(D, Q, j) for all g (hρ), c' \colon \Gamma \to \mathbb{Z}/\ell^\nu a homomorphism (Lean's c), g \colon \mathbb{Z}/\ell^\nu \to \mathbb{Z}, b \in \operatorname{integralEnd}(D, w_Q), k \in \mathbb{Z}/\ell^\nu, i \in \mathbb{C}, B a \mathbb{C}-linear map from \operatorname{formsOfLevel}(D, w_Q) to the forms of weight \kappa with b(y) = B(\operatorname{weightedSum}(\rho, c')(g)(y)) for every y (hb), and f \colon \mathbb{Z}/\ell^\nu \to \mathbb{C} with f(x - k) - i\cdot f(x) = g(x) for all x (e; by default proved by simp). Suppose (h): for every eigensystem x of level w_Q, every additive character \psi \colon \mathbb{Z}/\ell^\nu \to \mathbb{C}^\times and every form z of level w_Q in the isotypic space of x with \rho(g')z = \psi(c'(g'))\cdot z for all g' \in \Gamma, z \ne 0 and \psi(k) \ne i, one has \iota_\ell^{-1}(r(x))\cdot z = \iota_\ell^{-1}(s(x))\cdot B(z). Then \operatorname{weightedSum}(\rho, c')(g)\cdot \operatorname{heckeAction}(D, w_Q)(r) = b\cdot \operatorname{heckeAction}(D, w_Q)(s) in \operatorname{integralEnd}(D, w_Q).
The argument in briefTest on a form y of an eigensystem x. The functions on \mathbb{Z}/\ell^\nu have the additive characters as a basis; the map T(\varphi) = \sum_g (\varphi(c'(g) - k) - i\varphi(c'(g)))\rho(g)y sends \psi to an eigenvector of character \psi^{-1}\circ c'. T(\psi) = (\psi^{-1}(k) - i)\cdot \sum_g \psi(c'(g))\rho(g)y vanishes whenever \psi^{-1}(k) = i, and may vanish also when \psi^{-1}(k) \ne i (for instance when y has no component in that character space); where T(\psi) = 0 the identity at \psi is trivial, and where T(\psi) \ne 0 one has \psi^{-1}(k) \ne i, so h gives the identity on that basis vector. Finally T(f) = \operatorname{weightedSum}(\rho, c')(g)(y) by e.
