What it saysLet p, \Gamma, k be as in the setting, p_a = \varphi^{a}(E) \in S for a \in \mathbb{N}, and n \in \mathbb{Z} with n \ne 0 (hn). Then there are g \in galMonoid(k), a unit \omega of A_{\mathrm{inf}} and \upsilon, X \in A_{\mathrm{inf}} such that: g\cdot p_a = \omega\cdot p_a; p_a divides g\cdot s - s for every s \in S; p_a divides \upsilon\cdot X - p^{\mathrm{cost}(a, n)}; and z\tau\cdot X = \omega^{n} - 1 (\tau = [\varepsilon^{1/p}] - 1, z the coordinate of the chart).
The argument in briefTake g \in \Gamma' with gz = [\varepsilon]^{p^{a}}z and write gE = \omega E; then z\tau divides \omega - 1, so \varphi^{a}(\omega^{n} - 1) = z\tau\cdot X. The bound \theta\varphi^{-a}(X) | p^{\mathrm{cost}(a,n)} comes from lifting the exponent (\theta(\omega^{n} - 1) | p^{v_p(n)}\theta(\omega - 1)) and from comparing \theta(\omega - 1) with \theta of a derivative of E at z, which divides p^{d}, and with \theta([\varepsilon]^{p^{a}} - 1)z, which costs 3a.
