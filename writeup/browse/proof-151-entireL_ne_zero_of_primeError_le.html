lemma entireL_ne_zero_of_primeError_le

What it saysThe one-sided criterion. Let M be a natural number with M \ne 0 (instance NeZero M), a \in \mathbb{Z}/M and \beta a real number, with 1/2 < \beta (hβ) and a a unit of \mathbb{Z}/M (ha); assume (h) that for every prime d with d \equiv a mod M: d \bmod 8 = 7, M and d are coprime, and R_{d,M}(x) \le x^{\beta - 1} for all sufficiently large real x (the threshold may depend on d). Then for every complex number \rho with \beta < \operatorname{Re} \rho (h\rho, a strict inequality) and every Dirichlet character \chi modulo M: \widetilde{L}(\rho, \chi) \ne 0 (\widetilde{L} = entireL).
The argument in briefLandau's lemma for the polar function R_{d,M}(e^{v}), for a prime d \equiv a mod M chosen (exists_entireL_quadTwist_ne_zero) so that the twists \xi\cdot\chi_d of all characters \xi modulo M at once have no zero in \operatorname{Re} s \ge \beta, |\operatorname{Im} s| \le |\operatorname{Im} \rho|; the residue at \rho is then the sum of the orders of the \widetilde{L}(\cdot, \xi) at \rho, divided by \varphi(M)\cdot\rho(1 - \rho), and Landau's lemma makes it vanish.
