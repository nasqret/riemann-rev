lemma IsJointlyInvariant.sum_chain_mem_nsmul

What it saysLet E be a field with a CM type, k_j theta kernels as in chain, M a complex vector space, \varphi a form of stage 0, X a type, m \in \mathbb{N}, s \subseteq X finite, L_x linear maps into M and \Phi_x data. Assume H: IsJointlyInvariant(k, m, s, L, \Phi); fr: for j < m and every g the stabiliser classAut(K_j, g) of the class gK_j in the rational points is trivial; h\varphi: \varphi is fixed by K_0; P_j(g, v) properties with hs: for x \in s, j < m, points g, h and vectors v, P_j(g, v) implies P_{j+1}(h, \mathrm{jetMatrix}_j(\omega_j(g, h)\Phi_{x,j})v); hB: P_0(g, \varphi(g)) for every g; and, for a point h and a set A \subseteq M, hL: for every linear \beta, if P_m(h, \beta(\otimes\Phi_x)) for all x \in s then \sum_{x\in s} L_x(\beta(\otimes\Phi_x)) \in A. Then \sum_{x\in s} L_x(\operatorname{chain}(k, \Phi_x, m)(\varphi)(h)) lies in c\cdot A, the set of sums of c elements of A, where c = \prod_{j<m} |\Gamma_j\backslash G_f(n_j)/K_j| is the product of the numbers of classes.
The argument in briefInduction on m. A chain value at stage m + 1 is a class integral over stage m; by joint invariance the integrand is right K_m-invariant, by the induction hypothesis its values lie in the smaller sumset, and with free stabilisers the integral is a sum over the classes.
